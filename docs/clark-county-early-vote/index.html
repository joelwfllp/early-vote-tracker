Clark County's early vote: who is voting, not just how many

Ballots cast in Clark County, Nevada, by registered party, past voting, and age, compared with the same point in 2024 and 2022. Data through 2026-10-20 (14 days before Election Day). Updated daily from the county's public files.

So far
  • Democrats lead Republicans by 10,353 ballots (111,183 cast), against 5,298 at this point in 2024. Counting only mail ballots, the only kind with daily 2022 data, the lead is 8,757 vs. 12,358 in 2022.
  • Against 2024, every group is leaning more Democratic, irregular voters most of all. Among voters who cast a ballot in only one of the last two generals, Democrats lead by +11.3 points of the group's ballots, against +2.5 at this point in 2024; among voters who skipped both, +16.6 vs. -1.3. Regular voters, 72% of ballots so far, are at +8.6 vs. +2.7.
  • Against 2022, on mail ballots, every group is leaning less Democratic. Voters who cast a ballot in one of the last two generals: +9.8 vs. +17.6; regular voters: +17.2 vs. +31.7. The reason is Republican mail ballots: up 39% from this point in 2022, against +4% for Democrats. Whether that is Republicans shifting to mail or turning out more, the data cannot say yet; in-person early votes from 2022 were not archived.
  • So the early vote alone cannot say what the final electorate will look like. Finishing at 2024's pace and at 2022's mail pace give answers nearly 20 points apart on the Democratic-Republican margin. What it can show is who is turning out, group by group.

The running lead

A raw lead says how many more Democrats than Republicans have voted. It does not say whether those are voters who would have voted anyway, which is the question the rest of this page takes up.

Each voter is grouped by whether they voted in the last two general elections (2022 and 2024 for this year's voters). "Registered since the last general" is mostly new and automatically registered voters.

Each row assumes every party and voting-history group finishes at the share of its eventual turnout that it had reached at this point in a past election: 2024 (all early ballots) or 2022 (mail ballots only). The two give very different electorates, because each mostly encodes how that year's voters split between mail, early in-person, and Election Day voting, and that split has shifted, especially for Republicans. Clark has only two comparable elections since Nevada began mailing every active voter a ballot in 2020, too few to test which assumption is right.

Shares are of all ballots by party registration, not vote choice. 2022 and 2024 finals come from today's voter list, which drops voters removed since.

Method and caveats

Sources: Clark County Election Department daily in-person early-vote files, nightly mail-ballot file, and weekly voter list (party, birth year, registration date, and the last 20 elections with how each voter voted). 2024 comparisons use the county's 2024 in-person and mail files; 2022 uses its mail file. Mail ballots count once returned and accepted or awaiting a signature cure. Past turnout and age come from today's voter list, so for 2022 and 2024, voters since removed from the rolls are missing (3% of 2024's early and mail ballots, 9% of 2022's mail ballots), and party for in-person 2024 voters is their current registration. Each cycle is compared at the same number of days before Election Day; in-person early voting opened 17 days out in all three.
