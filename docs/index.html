Clark County's early vote: who is voting, not just how many

Ballots cast in Clark County, Nevada, by registered party, past voting, and age, compared with the same point in 2022 and 2024. Data through 2026-10-26 (8 days before Election Day). Updated daily from the county's public files.

So far
  • Democrats lead Republicans by 34,903 ballots (289,194 cast, +12.1 points), against 17,100 (+7.2) at this point in 2022 and 2,470 (+0.5) in 2024.
  • Regular voters look like 2022. Among voters who cast ballots in both of the last two generals, 71% of ballots so far, Democrats lead by +10.6 points of the group's ballots, against +8.3 in 2022 and +0.6 in 2024.
  • The difference is irregular and younger voters. Voters who cast a ballot in only one of the last two generals are at +16.5 (2022: +3.9); voters who skipped both, +22.7 (+6.0). Voters 18 to 29 are at +36.9 (+23.7) and 30 to 44 at +30.1 (+13.2), while voters 65 and older are less Democratic than in 2022 (+4.6 vs. +6.3).
  • That is a turnout story, not a forecast. Finishing at 2022's pace and at 2024's pace within each party and voting-history group gives electorates 7 points apart on the Democratic-Republican margin, and Clark has too few comparable elections to test which is right.

The running lead

A raw lead says how many more Democrats than Republicans have voted. It does not say whether those are voters who would have voted anyway, which is the question the rest of this page takes up.

Each row assumes every party and voting-history group finishes at the share of its eventual turnout that it had reached at this point in a past election. The two give different electorates, because each mostly encodes how that year's voters split between early and Election Day voting, and that split has shifted, especially for Republicans, who moved from Election Day to in-person early voting between 2022 and 2024. Clark has only two comparable elections since Nevada began mailing every active voter a ballot in 2020, too few to test which assumption is right.

Shares are of all ballots by party registration, not vote choice. 2022 and 2024 finals come from today's voter list, which drops voters removed since.

Method and caveats

Sources: Clark County Election Department daily in-person early-vote files, nightly mail-ballot file, and weekly voter list (party, birth year, registration date, and the last 20 elections with how each voter voted). 2022 and 2024 comparisons use the county's in-person and mail files for those elections. Mail ballots count once returned and accepted or awaiting a signature cure. 2018 and 2020 in-person files are no longer available, so those years are not compared. Past turnout and age come from today's voter list, so for 2022 and 2024, voters since removed from the rolls are missing (3% of 2024's early and mail ballots, 8% of 2022's), and party for in-person 2022 and 2024 voters is their current registration (those files carry the ballot party, not registration). Each cycle is compared at the same number of days before Election Day; in-person early voting opened 17 days out in all three.
